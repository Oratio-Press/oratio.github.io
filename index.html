A deeper prayer life needs a clear place to begin.

Many Christians want a deeper and more consistent life of Scripture and prayer, but they are not sure where to start.

A simple devotional routines may leave you without depth. A historic prayer office may introduce terms and prayers you have not used before. Oratio begins with Scripture and guided prayer. From there, you can add the Psalms, fixed prayer times, and fuller prayer offices.

Choose the Prayer Form that fits your present life.

Five Prayer Forms let you choose how much Scripture, Psalmody, and prayers to include. Use one Prayer Form consistently, or change forms when your time and needs change.

  1. Scripture & Prayer

    Simple Scripture reading and guided prayer, following Luther's Simple Way to Pray.

  2. Psalms & Prayer

    Add a Psalm of the Day, an opening versicle, a short reading, and a closing collect.

  3. Daily Prayer

    Pray a simple Morning, Evening, or Night Office with Psalms, readings, canticles, the Lord’s Prayer, and the Collect of the Day.

  4. The Daily Offices

    Pray fuller Matins, Midday Prayer, Vespers, and Compline with seasonal material, hymns, responsories, canticles, and collects.

  5. The Full Daily Office

    Pray the complete Daily Office and its seasonal variations, as found in the Brotherhood Prayer Book.

You can stay with one Prayer Form or change it whenever your circumstances change. How the Prayer Forms work

Built around Scripture and prayer.

Oratio puts Scripture and prayer first. Its readings, prayers, Church Year material, prayer records, and teaching notes help you return to that practice each day.

Scripture at the center

Begin with God's Word and guided prayer, following Luther's Simple Way to Pray.

Sustainable practice

Grow into historic Christian prayer at a pace that fits your life.

Pray throughout the day

Pray in the morning and evening, then add other offices when your schedule allows.

The Church Year

Pray with the Church Year through seasonal readings, colors, collects, hymns, and teaching notes.

Guidance and teaching

Teaching notes explain terms such as versicle, collect, antiphon, and responsory as they appear.

Prayer records

Prayer records let you review your prayer history and build discipline.

Rooted in historic Christianity

Oratio is rooted in confessional Lutheran Christianity and the historic Daily Office. It is for Christians who want to pray Scripture daily and learn more about historic Christian prayer.
